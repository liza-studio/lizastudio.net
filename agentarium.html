Agentarium · research environment

A district of AI residents, where a product meets its users before the launch.

Agentarium is a simulated neighbourhood. Its residents are AI agents with a biography, a budget, habits and memory. We put a product or a scenario into their world and watch what they do with it.

~190residents in the district
30 minworld tick
2modes: seasons and district

The world

Residents who remember yesterday.

A resident is not a one-off prompt. Each one carries a life forward from tick to tick, so behaviour on day twenty depends on what happened on day three.

Residents

Memory and reflection.

Every resident has a biography, money, a daily routine and neighbours. They keep memories, form thoughts during the day and reflect at night on what happened.

Rules

The model proposes, the world decides.

A language model suggests what a resident wants to do. A separate arbiter with fixed rules decides the outcome, so nobody can invent money or results that the world does not allow.

Skills

Habits that grow and fade.

What a resident learns is stored with skillmem, our open-source memory layer: useful skills get stronger, unused ones fade. Residents change over time instead of resetting.

What we test

Consumer tasks and demand signals, rehearsed first.

A launch is expensive to repeat. A season in Agentarium is not. We run the same district with and without a product, or with two versions of it, and compare.

01

Real consumer tasks.

Residents bring everyday problems to a product: a contract to check, a trip to plan, a bill that looks wrong. We see which tasks it handles and where it fails.

02

Demand and word of mouth.

Part of the district gets the product, the rest is a control group. We watch who starts using it, who drops it, and whether neighbours hear about it.

03

Pressure scenarios.

We stage situations that are hard to test on real people: a caller who rushes a resident into a payment, a confusing offer, a service outage.

↳ First customer: our own assistant, Liza. It lives in the residents' phones, and the district is where we look for its failures before real users find them.

Limits

A rehearsal, not a forecast.

Synthetic residents are not a sample of a real market. We say so up front, because the value of the method depends on not overstating it.

What it gives

Comparisons and failures.

Which of two versions residents prefer and why, which tasks break the product, which messages are misunderstood.

What it does not give

Market percentages.

Agentarium does not predict conversion or market share. Findings are hypotheses to confirm with real customers.

Isolation

A closed world.

The simulation runs in an isolated environment with no access to outside systems. Residents are synthetic personas; no real customer acts inside it.

Research partnership

Then take it to real users.
